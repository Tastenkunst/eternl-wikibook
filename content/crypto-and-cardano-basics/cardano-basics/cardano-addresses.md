# Cardano Addresses

A Cardano wallet can manage many addresses. A **payment address** is used to receive and control ADA and native tokens. A **stake address**, also called a reward address, identifies a staking credential used for delegation and rewards.

Addresses and credentials are public information. Sharing an address allows others to send funds to it or look up associated on-chain activity. A seed phrase or private key is different: it must be kept secret.

For example, to receive ADA, you can share a payment address generated by your wallet. When you later want to send those funds, the transaction is signed using the corresponding private key, either by a software wallet or by a hardware wallet. Your **seed phrase** is a backup that can be used to restore access to your wallet; unlike an address, it must never be shared. Learn more in [What Is a Seed Phrase?](/crypto-and-cardano-basics/security-and-privacy/what-is-a-seed-phrase.md).

## Payment Addresses and Staking

A payment address contains a payment credential that determines the conditions for spending funds at that address. For a typical wallet, a transaction must be signed with the corresponding key. Addresses can also use scripts with other spending conditions.

Many payment addresses also contain a reference to a staking credential. When that credential is delegated to a stake pool, eligible ADA at associated addresses contributes to the delegated stake. The ADA is not sent to the pool and remains spendable. Balances are recorded at snapshots taken during the delegation and reward cycle.

Registering and delegating the staking credential does not require each associated payment address to be registered separately. A wallet can generate additional payment addresses associated with that credential.

Multiple payment addresses can refer to the same staking credential. The payment and staking credentials of an address can also be controlled by different parties. Such addresses are sometimes called **hybrid** or **Franken addresses**: the party that can spend the funds is not necessarily the party that controls their staking rights.

For more about delegation and rewards, see [How Staking Works](/crypto-and-cardano-basics/cardano-basics/how-staking-works.md).

## Common Address Types

### Base Addresses

A base address contains both a payment credential and a staking credential. ADA at the address can contribute to stake delegated through that staking credential.

### Enterprise Addresses

An enterprise address has a payment credential but no staking credential. ADA at that address therefore does not contribute to a delegated stake through that address. Enterprise addresses can be used by individuals as well as organizations.

### Pointer Addresses

A pointer address refers indirectly to a staking credential through an on-chain registration certificate. New pointer addresses cannot be added to Cardano mainnet under the current ledger rules, but existing ones are part of the address format.

### Stake or Reward Addresses

A stake address identifies a staking credential and its reward account. It is used for staking-related operations and rewards; it is not a payment address for receiving ordinary ADA or token transfers.

### Byron Addresses

Byron addresses are an older Cardano address format. They look different from the newer Shelley-era formats and remain relevant for older wallets.

## Recognizing Addresses

Common mainnet payment addresses begin with `addr1`, and mainnet stake addresses with `stake1`. Testnet addresses use prefixes such as `addr_test1` and `stake_test1`. Byron addresses use a different encoding and do not follow these prefixes.

Before sending funds, check the address and network carefully. A valid-looking address does not prove that it belongs to the intended recipient.
